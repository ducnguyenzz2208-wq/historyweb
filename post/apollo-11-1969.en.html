On July 20, 1969, hundreds of millions of people around the planet held their breath watching the screen. For the first time in history, humans are about to set foot on another celestial body.

Journey to the Moon

Apollo 11 left the launch pad on July 16, carrying three astronauts and the dreams of all humanity. After four days of traversing endless space, the Eagle module separated and landed in the Sea of ​​Tranquility.

The eagle has landed.

Historic footsteps

When stepping down onto the surface of the Moon, the astronaut uttered the words that went down in history:

"One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."

In a few short hours, they collected soil and rock samples, planted flags and left behind footprints that — without wind — would last for millions of years.

Why is this important?

Apollo 11 was not just a triumph of science and technology. It is a testament to the miracles that people can do when they dare to dream big enough.

Looking at the Moon tonight, remember that there are still our footprints there.
