The Trung Sisters' Uprising

In AD 40, two daughters of a Me Linh chieftain raised the banner and won three years of self-rule amid a millennium of Chinese domination.

In 40 AD, two sisters and the only children of General Lac in Me Linh district raised a flag to rebel against the colonial government of the Eastern Han Dynasty. In just a short time, the movement spread throughout Giao Chi, Cuu Chan, Nhat Nam and Hop Pho; Governor To Dinh had to leave his headquarters and flee to the North. Trung Trac ascended the throne and established his capital in his hometown. That autonomy lasted only three years, but it was the first large-scale uprising recorded in official history in a thousand years of Chinese rule — and one led by women.1

Giao Chi under Eastern Han domination

After the Han Dynasty annexed Nam Viet in 111 BC, the land of the ancient Vietnamese was divided into districts, but for more than the first century, the Northern government still ruled indirectly: the native Lac generals kept the land, people and hereditary rights.

By the beginning of the 1st century, the policy changed direction. The colonial government promoted Sinicization, increased taxes and tribute, and gradually tightened the power of the Lac Viet aristocracy - the very class on which it had previously relied to rule. History records To Dinh as a "greedy and brutal" governor, but the deeper contradiction lies in the fact that the indigenous leadership class is being stripped of its power base.2

Three years of autonomy

The uprising broke out in the spring of 40. According to Dai Viet Complete History, Trung Trac raised the flag after her husband Thi Sach was killed by To Dinh; Earlier Chinese historical records did not record this detail, and modern researchers are inclined to read that the political motives of the Lac generals were the fundamental cause.

Recorded developments include several key milestones:

  • Insurgents from Me Linh invaded Luy Lau - the headquarters of the colonial government. To Dinh cut his hair, shaved his beard, and fled to Nam Hai.
  • The movement spread to 65 strongholds in a wide area, from Giao Chi down to Cuu Chan and all the way to Hop Pho.
  • Trung Trac proclaimed himself king, set up his capital in Me Linh, and exempted the people from taxes for two years.

In 42 AD, Emperor Quang Vu of Han sent the veteran general Ma Vien - titled General Phuc Ba - to lead his army south. The expeditionary force has an overwhelming advantage in organization and equipment. After major battles in the Lang Bac and Cam Khe regions, the insurgent force broke up; The two women died in the year 43. Folk legend says that the two women threw themselves into the Hat River to keep their spirits up.3

Why a military defeat is still a major milestone

After the victory, Ma Vien abolished the Lac general regime, placed Han mandarins down to the district level, relocated a part of the native leaders to China and had bronze pillars erected as landmarks. In other words, the Hai Ba Trung uprising marked the end of the indirect rule model — after 1943, the North truly became direct domination.

But that is also why this event remains in the collective memory as evidence that a sense of autonomy existed very early, before the Dai Viet state for dozens of centuries.4 The Hai Ba temple system in Me Linh, Hat Mon and Hanoi was continuously maintained through dynasties; Today the two women are still worshiped as protective deities, and a central district of the capital is named after them.
