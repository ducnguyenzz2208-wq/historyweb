At noon on April 30, 1975, tanks of the Liberation Army rammed the gate of the Independence Palace in Saigon. A few minutes later, President Duong Van Minh - who had only been in office for two days - read on the radio the surrender announcement. The Vietnam War ended; With it came thirty years of continuous conflict since 1945, and the division of the two regions stretching back to the 1954 Geneva Accords.1

The Paris Agreement and the changing balance

On January 27, 1973, the Paris Agreement was signed. US troops withdrew from the South within 60 days; American prisoners of war were returned. But the agreement left a key point: the Northern armed forces stationed in the South did not have to withdraw. In reality, the war transitioned into a period of direct confrontation between the two Vietnamese sides, with US aid to Saigon sharply reduced due to budget cuts by the US Congress after Watergate.2

By the end of 1974, the economic and military situation of the Republic of Vietnam deteriorated rapidly: there was a shortage of fuel, ammunition and spare parts, while inflation and a wave of desertions weakened soldiers' morale. The test battle in Phuoc Long in late 1974 - early 1975 showed that the United States would not return to intervene with air force. The Politburo in Hanoi decided to accelerate the plan.

Fifty-five days and nights

The Spring 1975 campaign took place faster than either side expected:

  • March 10 — Buon Ma Thuot: opening attack on the Central Highlands. After losing the town, President Nguyen Van Thieu ordered the withdrawal of the Central Highlands; The withdrawal along Route 7 turned into a disaster.
  • March 26 — Hue; March 29—Da Nang: the entire Military Region I disintegrates within days, sending a huge wave of evacuees south.
  • April 9–21 — Xuan Loc: the fiercest defensive battle of the Saigon army at the northeast gate. When this line broke, the road to the capital was left open. Nguyen Van Thieu resigned on April 21.
  • April 26–30 — Ho Chi Minh Campaign: five armies advance on Saigon. On April 29, the United States launched the largest helicopter evacuation campaign in history, flying more than 7,000 people out of the city in less than a day.

At noon on April 30, the leading units arrived at the center. The image of a tank passing through the gates of the Independence Palace became an iconic photo of the 20th century in Asia.

The price and what comes after

Casualty statistics are debated, but widely cited estimates for the entire 1955–1975 period hover around more than three million deaths in all three Indochina countries, the majority of whom were Vietnamese civilians.3 Millions more were left homeless; The consequences of landmines and toxic chemicals last until today.

On the state front, the unification process was completed on July 2, 1976, when the Joint National Assembly declared the establishment of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. The following years were a difficult period: industrial and commercial reform, embargo, war on the Southwest and Northern borders, and a wave of people crossing the sea. It was not until Doi Moi in 1986 that the country entered a different trajectory.4

April 30 therefore has different names depending on the narrator's location - Southern Liberation Day, Reunification Day, or Black April in the overseas Vietnamese community. That multiplicity of meanings is itself a part of the history of this event.
