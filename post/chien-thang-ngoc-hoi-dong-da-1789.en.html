The Victory at Ngoc Hoi – Dong Da

Quang Trung promised his troops a Lunar New Year feast in Thang Long by the seventh day; he arrived two days early.

On the 5th day of the Year of the Rooster, January 30, 1789, the Tay Son army entered Thang Long. The Qing army stationed in the capital the day before had celebrated Tet in the posture of victors; At noon that day, Commander Ton Si Nghi abandoned his seal, crossed the Nhi River pontoon bridge and ran to the North. The entire campaign — from the time the army departed in Phu Xuan to the liberation of the capital — took place in about a month, and the decisive battle was wrapped up in five days of Tet.1

Why were the Qing army present in Thang Long?

At the end of the 18th century, Dang Ngoai fell into crisis: the government of King Le - Lord Trinh collapsed before the Tay Son movement. King Le Chieu Thong fled to China for help. The Qing Dynasty accepted — not necessarily out of obligation to a vassal, but out of the opportunity to regain influence in the South. At the end of 1788, Governor of Luong Quang Ton Si Nghi sent troops across the border and occupied Thang Long with almost no resistance.

Vietnamese history records the number of troops as 290,000; Modern research based on Qing Dynasty documents suggests that the actual fighting force was much smaller, with the rest being civilian laborers. 2 Whichever estimate is correct, the correlation is still unfavorable for the Tay Son — and even more unfavorable is time: the Tay Son army was then stationed in Phu Xuan, more than 600 km by road from Thang Long.

Speedy march and Ngoc Hoi battle

On December 22, 1788, Nguyen Hue ascended the throne, established the reign name Quang Trung, and immediately marched to the North. Arriving in Nghe An, he stopped to recruit more soldiers and review the army, then continued to advance at a speed that history books still record as a logistical miracle: soldiers took turns resting on hammocks, moving in formation day and night.

Before the shooting, Quang Trung arranged with his three troops to celebrate Tet in Thang Long on the 7th. In fact, he returned two days earlier. The attack plan is divided into several coordinated prongs:

  1. Front front: captured the forward posts south of the capital on New Year's Eve and the 3rd, cutting off the news route to Thang Long.
  2. Ngoc Hoi (5th day of Tet): Strongest base of the Qing army. Tay Son used clapboards wrapped in wet straw as shields against fire, and the infantry followed; The post was taken down in the morning, Admiral Hua The Hanh died in battle.
  3. Dong Da (same day): The army led by Admiral Long attacked the camp in Khuong Thuong. General Sam Nghi Dong hanged himself; This direction opens straight into the southwest gate of the capital.

Being attacked from two sides at the same time while still believing that the enemy was still far away, the Qing army command lost the ability to react. The chaotic retreat across the Nhi River broke the pontoon bridge and caused more casualties than the fighting.

After victory: a diplomatic move

It is worth noting that Quang Trung did not exploit his victory to the end. Immediately after taking control of Thang Long, he sent an embassy to Beijing to ask for peace, using a nominal surrender ceremony in exchange for the Qing dynasty's recognition of the new government and abandoning the idea of ​​restoring the Le dynasty.3 This policy kept peace on the northern border for the remainder of the Tay Son dynasty.

That dynasty did not last long — Quang Trung died in 1792 at the age of 39, and the Tay Son dynasty collapsed in 1802. But the Ky Dau campaign is still studied as a model of military speed, secrecy and multi-directional contracting. 4 Dong Da Mound in Hanoi is still held every year on the 5th day of Tet.
