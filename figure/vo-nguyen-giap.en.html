From a history teacher without formal military training, Vo Nguyen Giap (1911–2013) became the first general of the Vietnam People's Army and one of the most studied military commanders of the 20th century — the man who associated his name with the Dien Bien Phu victory.

The teacher leads the army

Born in Quang Binh, Vo Nguyen Giap studied law and taught history in Hanoi before participating in revolutionary activities. He was not trained at any military academy; Knowledge of the art of war comes largely from self-study, from the history of the nation's resistance wars and from battlefield practice. In 1944, he commanded the Vietnam Liberation Propaganda Army — the predecessor of the regular army — with only 34 people.1

Dien Bien Phu and the art of logistics

The pinnacle of his career was the Dien Bien Phu campaign in 1954. His most famous decision here was not an attack, but the postponement of the attack at the last moment: switching from "fight quickly, win quickly" to "fight firmly, advance firmly", pulling out the artillery to reorganize. Victory relied more on a logistical feat — tens of thousands of civilians carrying artillery and food through the mountains and forests — than on firepower superiority.2

From the resistance against the French to the resistance against the US

After 1954, he continued to play a role in military leadership throughout the resistance war against the United States, although the degree of direct participation in each campaign is a subject of debate among historians. In his later years, he became a widely respected icon, and spoke out on civic issues such as environmental protection. He passed away in 2013, at the age of 102, in the grief of many people.3

Analysis

What makes Vo Nguyen Giap studied in Western military academies is not just his victories, but the way he applied people's war: linking military with politics, logistics and the will of the masses, turning weaknesses in equipment into strengths in perseverance. He also exemplifies the thesis that in a war of liberation, the materially weaker side does not need to win militarily — it just needs to not lose and prolong it until the cost becomes too great for the opponent. The campaign that made his name is told in the Dien Bien Phu Victory.
