In Vietnam's military history, there are few names associated with persistent victory like Tran Hung Dao - the National Duke Tiet who three times commanded Dai Viet's army and people to defeat the Yuan-Mongol empire, the most powerful army in the world in the 13th century.

Three resistances

Faced with a much more numerous and skilled enemy, he did not choose a direct confrontation. The "empty garden" plan, retreating to preserve forces and then counterattacking at the right time, turned the enemy's superiority into a burden. The climax was the Bach Dang battle in 1288, when an underground pile submerged the enemy's warships.

Thoughts left behind

He is the author of Hich Tuong Si - a poem that arouses the self-esteem and will of soldiers - and Essentials of War summarizing the art of military leadership.

Analysis

Tran Hung Dao's greatest legacy is the lesson "drill the strength of the people to make a deep-rooted plan": military power is only strong when it is based on the people's hearts. He was revered by later generations as Saint Tran, a respect that went beyond the scope of a general.
