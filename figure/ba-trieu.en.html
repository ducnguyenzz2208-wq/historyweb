Lady Trieu (225–248 AD), named Trieu Thi Trinh and honorifically revered as Trieu Trinh Nuong, stands among the greatest female national heroes in Vietnamese history. In the 3rd century AD, she raised the banner of armed rebellion against the oppressive yoke of China's Eastern Wu dynasty. Her legendary proclamation — "I wish to ride the tempest, tread the roaring waves..." — remains an immortal manifesto of female courage and national self-determination.

1. Daughter of the Mountains of Cuu Chan

Trieu Thi Trinh was born in 225 AD in the mountainous highlands of Quan Trieu, Cuu Chan prefecture (modern-day Thanh Hoa province). Orphaned at an early age, she was raised by her older brother, Trieu Quoc Dat, an influential local chieftain.

Growing up under foreign imperial domination and witnessing the brutal exploitation inflicted upon her people by Eastern Wu magistrates, she harbored an intense desire to drive out the oppressors. When well-meaning elders urged her to accept traditional domestic domesticity and marriage, the twenty-year-old woman delivered an answer that reverberates through the ages:

"I wish to ride the tempest, tread the roaring waves, slay the giant whales of the Eastern Sea, reclaim the realm, establish our independence, and cast off the chains of servitude, not to bow my head and serve as a humble concubine to anyone!"

2. The Golden-Armored Commander on the War Elephant

In 248 AD, with widespread popular support, Lady Trieu and her brother mobilized their followers from their mountain redoubt on Nua Mountain. Thousands of volunteers from across the region flocked to join their righteous cause.

When Trieu Quoc Dat passed away, the insurgent army unanimously elected her Supreme Commander. On the battlefield, clad in resplendent golden armor, wearing ivory clogs, her hair crowned with a golden hairpin, and mounted atop a fierce single-tusked white war elephant, she directed tactical strikes with formidable grace. The Eastern Wu troops trembled at the trumpet of her war elephant, singing:

"To grapple with a tiger with bare hands is easy, To face the Lady King in armor is perilous indeed!"

Under her command, the rebels captured the regional administrative capital of Tu Pho, dismantled imperial garrisons, and liberated Cuu Chan, sending tremors of rebellion throughout Giao Chi.

3. Heroic Martyrdom on Tung Mountain

Alarmed by the uprising, the court of Eastern Wu dispatched experienced general Lu Yin with tens of thousands of veteran soldiers to suppress the rebellion. Facing overwhelming numbers and insidious political intrigue designed to bribe local chiefs, Lady Trieu fought fiercely across more than thirty engagements.

In the autumn of 248, during a desperate final stand at Tung Mountain (Hau Loc, Thanh Hoa), facing encirclement and refusing dishonorable capture, Lady Trieu took her own life at the tender age of 23, preserving her sacred honor.

4. An Eternal Icon of the Vietnamese Spirit

Though the uprising was quelled, Lady Trieu riding her war elephant into battle transcended mortal history to become an enduring mythological archetype of liberty. Honored with temples at Tung Mountain and bestowed with imperial posthumous titles across successive dynasties, her memory lives vibrantly in the soul of the nation as a beacon of indomitable courage and female empowerment.
