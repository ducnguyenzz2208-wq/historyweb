Abraham Lincoln led America through the greatest challenge in its history — the Civil War — and in the process redefined the very meaning of nationhood.

Origin

Born into a poor, almost self-educated family on the frontier, Lincoln exemplified the idea that status need not be inherited. The path from small-town lawyer to the White House has become part of American legend.

Maintain the Union

When the Southern states seceded in 1861, Lincoln bet his entire term on one belief: a nation "of the people, by the people, for the people" must not be allowed to disintegrate. The Emancipation Proclamation (1863) turned the fight to preserve the Union into a fight for freedom.

Analysis

Lincoln's strength lay in his words and moral steadfastness. The Gettysburg Address, only a few minutes long, condensed democratic ideals into words that are remembered today. He was assassinated in 1865, just a few days after the war ended, right when facing an equally difficult challenge: healing a divided country. That unfinished legacy makes him one of the most pondered figures in modern history.
