On September 2, 1945, at Ba Dinh Square, Ho Chi Minh read the Declaration of Independence, giving birth to the Democratic Republic of Vietnam. That moment was the convergence of nearly four decades of relentless revolutionary activity.

Journey to find your way

Leaving Nha Rong wharf in 1911 with the name Van Ba, he traveled to many continents, doing many jobs to live and study. It was the experience of colonial people everywhere that shaped the path he chose: linking national independence with human liberation.

The creator

Returning to the country in 1941, he founded the Viet Minh Front, gathering all classes into a common goal. His power did not lie in weapons, but in the ability to turn a vague aspiration into an organized movement.

Analysis

What made Ho Chi Minh a historical figure was not just his victory, but the way he put the issue of independence into a narrative in which millions of people could recognize themselves. He died in 1969, before the country was unified, but the symbol he left behind is still at the heart of the nation's memory.
