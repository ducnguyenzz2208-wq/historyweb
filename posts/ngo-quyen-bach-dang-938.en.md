# Bach Dang 938: Ngo Quyen and the end of a thousand years of Northern domination

At the end of 938, on the Bach Dang River estuary, Ngo Quyen used an underground pile battle to sink the warships of the Southern Han army, killing the general Liu Hoang Thao. That victory ended more than a thousand years of Northern domination, opening a long-term era of independence and autonomy for the Vietnamese people. If the Hai Ba Trung uprising was the earliest voice of independence, then Bach Dang 938 was the milestone when that independence became a lasting reality.[^1]

## Context: the power vacuum after the Tang Dynasty

In the early 10th century, the Tang Dynasty in China collapsed and disintegrated, ushering in the chaotic Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. In An Nam, the Khuc family and then the Duong family took the opportunity to gain de facto autonomy, although in name they were still part of the Northern territory.

In 937, Kieu Cong Tien killed Duong Dinh Nghe to seize power, then when Ngo Quyen - a general and also Duong Dinh Nghe's son-in-law - summoned troops to question his guilt, he asked for help from the Southern Han dynasty in Guangzhou. King Nam Han took advantage of this excuse and sent his son Luu Hoang Thao to bring his navy to plot to annex Vietnamese land.[^2]

## Stake battle on Bach Dang river

Ngo Quyen quickly destroyed Kieu Cong Tien, then focused on dealing with the oncoming army. He chose the Bach Dang estuary as the decisive battle place and devised a plan to exploit the law of tides - later becoming a model followed by Le Hoan (981) and Tran Hung Dao (1288).

The battle consists of three closely coordinated steps in time:

* **Picking:** The army and people placed large wooden stakes, with sharp ends covered with iron, into the river bed at a dangerous section; When the tide rises, the piles sink below the water's surface.
* **Luring the enemy:** When the water was strong, Ngo Quyen sent out light boats to challenge and then pretended to lose, luring the Nam Han fleet to cross the pile and go deeper inside.
* **Counterattack:** When the tide began to recede, the ambush army poured out to attack. The enemy boats awkwardly backed away and crashed into a raised pile, broke and sank en masse. Liu Hongcao died in battle; King Nam Han, who was far away, heard the news and withdrew his troops.[^3]

## Meaning: from "district" to "country"

In 939, Ngo Quyen proclaimed himself king and set up his capital in Co Loa - the former capital of An Duong Vuong - as a declaration to resume the tradition of independent statehood during the country's founding. He abolished the position of Northern Governor and established his own court and officials.

Tran dynasty historian Le Van Huu later evaluated the Bach Dang victory as a milestone in "resuming national unity" for the Vietnamese people. Although the Ngo Dynasty existed for a short time and the country still experienced the chaos of the twelve warlords, the 938 mark was still considered a definitive boundary: from here, independence became the normal state, and the Northern domination became the exception that every subsequent dynasty was determined to keep from recurring. [^4] The Bach Dang pile was also recreated three centuries later in the Bach Dang Victory in 1288, and the architect of this victory was Ngo Quyen.
