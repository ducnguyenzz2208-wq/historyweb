# French Revolution 1789: From budget crisis to national sovereignty

On July 14, 1789, the Parisian crowd occupied the fortress - the Bastille prison. Inside there were only seven prisoners, none of whom were famous political prisoners. In military terms, it was a minor event; Politically, it ended the illusion that the king still controlled his capital. Within six weeks, feudal privilege was abolished and France published a declaration affirming that humans are born free and equal in rights.[^1]

## Financial trigger

The revolution started not from prison but from accounting books. Financing the Seven Years' War and the American Revolution pushed the French treasury to the brink of insolvency; By the late 1780s, most budget revenues were just enough to pay interest. All tax reform efforts faced opposition from the clergy and nobility — two classes that were largely exempt from tax obligations.[^2]

The deadlock forced Louis XVI to convene a Congress of the Estates (États généraux) in May 1789, the first in 175 years. Controversy immediately arose over how to vote: per capita or per caste. On June 17, the Third Estate delegates declared themselves the **National Assembly**, representing the entire nation. Three days later, locked out of the meeting room, they gathered at the tennis court and vowed not to disperse until the constitution was drafted. Sovereignty had just been transferred — on paper — from the king to the nation.

## Summer 1789 and the breakdown of the old order

News that the king had gathered troops around Paris, combined with the peak price of bread after the failed harvest in 1788, pushed the city into a state of rebellion. Bastille was captured on July 14; Governor de Launay was killed. In the countryside, a wave of panic and riots called *Grande Peur* spread throughout the provinces, with peasants burning land rent books and occupying lords' castles.

Congress responded with two foundational documents:

* **Night of August 4:** abolished feudal privilege, tithes, and lord duties — ending on the principle of caste society.
* **August 26:** adopts the **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen**, establishing the rights to liberty, property, security, and the right against oppression; asserts that law is an expression of the general will.[^3]

In October, a group of Parisian women marched to Versailles and escorted the royal family to the capital. The king has since lived under the supervision of the city he once ruled.

## Why 1789 is still important

The French Revolution then passed through the republic, the Terror, the Directory and finally the Napoleonic empire — a trajectory that has left historians endlessly debating whether the violence of 1793–1794 was a necessary consequence or a deviation from the principles of 1789.[^4]

What is less controversial is the institutional legacy: the concept of citizens over subjects, equality before the law, a written constitution, and the idea that the legitimacy of government stems from the nation. Those principles followed the French army throughout Europe, then followed books and international students much further - including Vietnamese intellectuals in the early 20th century, who read the *Declaration of Human and Civil Rights* right in the language of the colonial government that was ruling them.
