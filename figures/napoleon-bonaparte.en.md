From an unknown artillery officer, Napoleon Bonaparte rose to become emperor of France and in a short time redrawn the map of Europe.

## Military leader

His military talent was demonstrated by his speed and daring: campaigns such as Austerlitz in 1805 became exemplary in military academies to this day. He knew how to concentrate his forces on the enemy's weak points and make decisions faster than the enemy.

## Reformer

Less remembered but more influential was the *Napoleonic Code* (1804), which codified civil law based on the principle of equality before the law. It became the foundation for the legal systems of many countries.

## Collapse

His expansionist ambitions led him to disaster: the Russian expedition in 1812 disintegrated his army, and the final defeat at Waterloo in 1815 ended his career. He died in exile on the island of Saint Helena.

## Analysis

Napoleon was a figure full of contradictions: he both spread the ideals of the French Revolution, declared himself emperor and caused a war that killed millions of people. His legacy reminds us that the same man can be both a reformer and a conqueror — and that history rarely judges simply.
