Nelson Mandela (1918–2013) was a South African revolutionary and politician who devoted his life to fighting against the apartheid regime. After 27 years in prison, he became South Africa's first black president and a global symbol of national reconciliation.

## From lawyer to prisoner

Born into a line of chiefs in the Eastern Cape, Mandela studied law and became one of the first black lawyers in Johannesburg. He joined the African National Congress (ANC), fighting against the apartheid system - a brutal racial discrimination regime that deprived most of the rights of the black majority. Initially following a non-violent path, he switched to supporting armed struggle after the government bloody suppressed peaceful protests.[^1]

## 27 years behind bars

In 1964, Mandela was sentenced to life imprisonment in the Rivonia trial. He spent 27 years in prison, most of it on harsh Robben Island. But instead of being forgotten, he gradually became a symbol of the anti-apartheid movement around the world; The slogan "Freedom for Mandela" rang out in many countries, and international pressure on the South African government grew.[^2]

## Reconciliation instead of revenge

Released in 1990, Mandela and President F. W. de Klerk negotiated a peaceful end to apartheid — an effort that earned them both the Nobel Peace Prize in 1993. In 1994, in the first multiracial election, he was elected president. What makes the world admire him is his choice: instead of revenge, he pursued reconciliation, establishing the Truth and Reconciliation Commission to heal the nation's wounds. He proactively left his seat after one term, a rare precedent in Africa.[^3]

## Analysis

Mandela's historical weight lies not only in the fact that he helped end apartheid, but in the way he did it. South Africa in the early 1990s stood on the brink of ethnic civil war; The transfer of power took place relatively peacefully largely thanks to his moral reputation and tolerance policy. Like Mahatma Gandhi — who was active in South Africa and influenced its tradition of struggle — Mandela showed that a liberation movement can pursue justice without falling into a spiral of revenge. However, researchers also note that he left behind a South Africa that still has deep economic inequality - an unfinished legacy that future generations must continue.
